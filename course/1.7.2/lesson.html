What this project practises
  • A priority queue (1.7.1) with container/heap and your own Less.
  • The merge of 1.5.2 generalised to k inputs: the heap picks the smallest head among k in O(log k).
  • Deterministic output: a tie-break rule that makes equal times come out in a known order.

The situation

kubectl logs on a deployment with 300 pods gives 300 logs, each in time order. To follow one request across pods you need a single timeline. Concatenating and sorting works, but costs O(N log N) and needs everything in memory at once; a k-way merge reads each log front to back, keeps only one entry per pod in the heap, and works just as well on files or streams. It is also the merge step that external sort (1.6.3) and log tools like stern or Loki's query engine perform.
