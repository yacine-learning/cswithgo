History

Like two pointers (A4), it is a technique without a single inventor: two pointers moving in the same direction. Its best-known use is in networking: in 1974 Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn published the design of TCP, with flow control by a window. The sender may have a window's worth of bytes in flight, and the window slides forward as acknowledgements come back.

The problem it fixes

"The average of the last 10 values", "how many events in the last minute", "the longest stretch without a repeat": recomputing each window from scratch costs O(n × k).

How it works

Keep the window's summary (a sum, a count, a set) up to date as it moves: add what enters on the right, remove what leaves on the left. Each element enters once and leaves once: O(n), whatever the window size.

Where it is used

  • TCP flow control: the window of bytes allowed in flight.
  • Moving averages of measurements; counting events over the last few seconds (exercise 1).
  • String algorithms: the longest run without a repeated element (exercise 2).
