History

Two pointers is a technique, not one invention. Its oldest and most famous use is the merge at the heart of merge sort, which John von Neumann wrote in 1945 for the EDVAC (A6): two sorted runs, one index in each, always take the smaller.

The problem it fixes

On sorted data, many questions look like they need two nested loops ("is there a pair that sums to x?", "merge these two lists"), O(n²).

How it works

Keep two indexes and move only one of them at each step, chosen so that no possible answer is skipped. Each index crosses the slice once: O(n). They can start at both ends and walk towards each other (pair sum), or both start at the beginning, one reading and one writing (removing duplicates in place).

Where it is used

  • Merging sorted data: merge sort, merging traces from two processors (exercise 2).
  • Databases: the "merge join" of two tables sorted on the same key.
  • Removing duplicates or filtering a slice in place, without a second array.
