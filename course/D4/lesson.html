History

The mathematics of waiting lines started with telephones: in 1909 Agner Krarup Erlang, of the Copenhagen Telephone Company, published his first study of how calls arrive and wait for a free line. Queues entered computing with time-sharing: in November 1961 Fernando Corbató's team at MIT demonstrated CTSS, several people typing at once on one IBM 709, each program getting a short slice of processor time in turn. Corbató received the 1990 Turing Award for it.

The problem it fixes

Work arrives faster than it can be done, or from several sources at once. It must wait, and be served in arrival order. Removing from the front of a slice is O(1), but the memory in front is never reused.

How it works

First in, first out. A ring buffer keeps the queue in a fixed array with two indexes, head and tail, that wrap around to 0 at the end: no shifting, no allocation, memory reused forever.

Where it is used

  • A buffered Go channel is a ring buffer (runtime/chan.go).
  • The scheduler's run queue: processes waiting for their turn on the processor.
  • Network cards and keyboards: bytes wait in a ring until the program reads them.
  • Breadth-first search (A10).
