History

In his 1946 design for the ACE computer, Alan Turing described two routines, BURY and UNBURY, to call subroutines and return from them with a stack of return addresses. In Munich in 1955, Friedrich Bauer and Klaus Samelson showed how a stack (their Keller, "cellar") lets a machine evaluate and translate formulas, and patented the method. It became the heart of compilers, and Bauer later received the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award for it.

The problem it fixes

Nested things must be closed in the reverse order they were opened: function calls, brackets, HTML tags, undo steps. Something has to remember what is open, innermost first.

How it works

Push on top, pop from the top: last in, first out. On a slice, both are O(1) at the end. When you meet an opener, push it; when you meet a closer, it must match the top.

Where it is used

  • The call stack: every function call pushes a frame, every return pops it (A2).
  • Parsers and compilers: brackets, nested blocks, expression evaluation.
  • Undo in an editor; Back in a browser.
  • Depth-first search (A11) is a stack, explicit or through recursion.
